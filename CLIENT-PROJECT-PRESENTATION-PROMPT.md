# Claude Design prompt — 3-project client comparison deck

How to use: open Claude Design, **attach your outline deck + all 3 brochures**, paste the prompt
below, and fill in every `[BRACKET]`. Delete bracket lines that don't apply.

---

## THE PROMPT (copy from here down)

Build me a client-facing presentation that helps one buyer choose between three
off-plan Dubai projects.

**Attached files**
1. My outline deck — this is the source of truth for *which points* to cover per project.
   Follow its point order and its section structure. Don't add new sections.
2. Three developer brochures — one per project. These are the source of truth for *facts*:
   prices, sizes, payment plans, handover dates, unit counts, amenities, location.

**Audience & goal**
- Client: [CLIENT NAME / PROFILE — e.g. first-time investor, yield-driven, AED 1.1M budget, 1BR]
- Their priority, in order: [1. yield / 2. capital appreciation / 3. handover timing / 4. …]
- Goal of the deck: they should be able to make a decision by the last slide. It is a
  decision aid, not a brochure dump.
- Deck gets [presented live by me / sent as a link for them to read alone] — so
  [keep slides sparse, I'll narrate / make slides self-explanatory].

**Deliverable**
- A single self-contained artifact, 16:9 slides, one screen per slide, keyboard
  arrow navigation, and clean when printed to PDF.
- Slide count: [12–16]. Do not exceed it — cut the weakest content instead.
- Language: [English]. Currency: AED, formatted like `AED 1,050,000` and `AED 1,465/sqft`.

**Structure**
1. Title — client name, date, "3 projects, one decision"
2. The brief — their budget, unit type, priorities, in their own words. One slide.
3. The three projects at a glance — one comparison table, the 6–8 metrics that
   actually differentiate them. This is the most important slide in the deck.
4. One slide per project (×3) — same layout, same metric order every time, so they
   compare by position not by reading. Cover exactly the points from my outline deck.
5. Head-to-head on each priority the client named — one slide per priority, with a
   clear winner called out on each.
6. Trade-offs — what each project costs them. Every project gets at least one honest
   negative. No project is perfect.
7. Recommendation — [PROJECT X] as the pick, 3 reasons, plus the one condition that
   would flip the decision to a different project.
8. Next steps — [book a viewing / reserve unit / request current price list],
   and my contact: [NAME · PHONE · @HANDLE].

**Content rules — important**
- Every number must come from the attached brochures. Do not estimate, round for
  aesthetics, or fill gaps from general knowledge.
- If a metric is missing for one project, print `Not disclosed` in that cell. Never
  leave a blank and never infer it from the other two.
- Anything that is identical across all three (e.g. a DLD waiver everyone offers) is a
  wash — mention once, then exclude it from scoring and comparison.
- Compare like with like: same unit type, same bedroom count, same payment-plan basis.
  If the brochures aren't comparable on a metric, say so on the slide rather than
  forcing the comparison.
- Flag structural differences the client won't spot themselves — total unit count in
  the building (resale/leasing competition at handover), % of capital due before
  handover, developer track record, service charge exposure.
- Give me a short "verify before presenting" list at the end of your reply (not on a
  slide) of anything you took from a brochure that could be stale — prices, handover
  dates, availability.

**Design**
- Colors: [brand primary hex] with [accent hex], on [cream/white]. Neutral greys for
  supporting text. Use one single accent color for "winner" highlights — nothing else
  competes with it.
- Fonts: [FONT — headings] / [FONT — body]. If unavailable, use a clean system stack.
- Each project keeps one consistent identity color across the whole deck so the client
  tracks it by color, not by name.
- Tables over paragraphs. Numbers right-aligned. No stock photos, no clip art, no
  gradients-as-decoration. Use brochure renders only where they add information.
- Charts only where a chart beats a table: payment plan cash-flow timeline, and
  price/sqft comparison. Nothing else.
- Readable projected on a screen from the back of a room: body text no smaller than
  the equivalent of 18pt.

**Tone**
Direct and advisory. Short lines. I'm the broker giving a professional read, not a
salesperson pushing inventory. No hype adjectives ("stunning", "iconic", "unmatched").

**Footer on every slide**
`Figures are developer-published and subject to change — verify on the current price
list and SPA. For information only, not financial advice.`

**Before you build:** if anything in my outline deck conflicts with a brochure, ask me
before assuming which one wins.
