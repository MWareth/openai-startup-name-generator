## SLIDE 2 — Developer

**Developer — Mashriq Elite Developments**

Mashriq Elite Developments is the property arm of **Al Mashriq Group**, a diversified house
with **over two decades** of operating history across real estate, project development and
telecommunications, with a network spanning the UAE, United States, Europe, Singapore and
Saudi Arabia. The group runs its own in-house design centre, so architecture, interiors and
delivery sit under one roof.

Floareá Skies is described in the brochure as **their fourth venture** — positioned as the
project meant to set their benchmark. Their track record to date is short but real: a
project launched September 2023 was **handed over August 2025**, with further launches in
June 2024 and February 2025 (*Grandé*), and upcoming chapters announced for Dubai Islands
and Dubailand.

**Say this plainly to the client:** this is a boutique developer, not a Binghatti or an
EMAAR. The upside is a small, low-density building and negotiable terms; the risk is
delivery record depth. Verify the escrow account, RERA registration and construction-linked
SPA milestones — that is the honest mitigation, and it's a stronger position than pretending
the risk isn't there.

> ⚠️ Brochure says "our **fourth** venture"; my earlier research recorded it as their 5th.
> Confirm which, and don't overstate the count on a slide.

## Positioning against Arancia (for the head-to-head slide)

| | Floareá Skies | Arancia at The Yards |
|---|---|---|
| Location | JVC — dense, central, proven | City of Arabia — inland, new community |
| Developer | Mashriq Elite (boutique, 4th project) | BEYOND / OMNIYAT (premium brand, deep group) |
| Handover | **Q3–Q4 2027** | Q1 2029 |
| Total units in building/phase | **192** | 272 |
| Entry 1BR all-in | ~AED 1,055,250 | ~AED 1,045,250 |
| Net yield (modelled) | **6.2%** | 6.1% |
| Capital before handover | 50% | **40%** |
| Furnished / fitted | **Yes — full fit-out package** | No |
| Dubai Mall | 25 min | CONFIRM |

**Where Floareá wins:** it hands over **18 months earlier** — rent starts in 2027, not 2029.
It arrives fitted, which shortens vacancy and lifts achievable rent in JVC's young-professional
tenant pool. Only 192 units in the building. JVC is a *proven* leasing market with real
absorption, and the entry price sits comfortably under AED 1.1M. Plus the Metro Purple Line
station is a genuine future catalyst.

**Where it loses, and say it:** boutique developer with a thin delivery record — the real
risk on this deal. JVC is Dubai's most crowded pipeline (16.8k–22k units to 2028), so rents
could soften 5–10% right at handover. 50% of capital lands as a bullet payment on handover
day vs Arancia's 40% spread to 2028. And no view premium — this is a yield play, not an
address play.

**The clean way to frame the pair for a client:** near-identical cheque, near-identical net
yield — so the decision isn't about the numbers, it's about **2027 fitted in a proven
market with a small developer** versus **2029 unfitted in an unproven community with a
premium developer**. That framing makes the choice obvious to whichever client you're
sitting with, and it's honest.
